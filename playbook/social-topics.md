# Social Topic Selection — quick, useful, saveable

The social session is not a shortened article. It starts from separate research and wins by giving someone one useful thing before they scroll away.

## The five-second gate

A topic qualifies only when all of these are true:

1. **Immediate payoff.** The viewer understands the problem or benefit from the cover and first tweet
2. **Small enough to use today.** One fix, shortcut, setting, check, comparison, warning, or surprising fact—not a curriculum
3. **Useful without homework.** No notebook, long setup, new framework, or prerequisite lesson
4. **Specific.** Name the exact tool, behavior, situation, or action
5. **Save or share energy.** A person would keep it for later, send it to a teammate, or think “wait, that’s actually useful”
6. **Verifiable.** The central claim is supported by a primary or otherwise strong source

## Good families

- Tiny debugging wins that remove a common annoyance
- Browser, terminal, editor, Git, or operating-system behaviors people use every day
- One security or privacy check with an immediate action
- Short before/after improvements to a frequent workflow
- A counterintuitive technical fact with a practical consequence
- One API, command, setting, or shortcut most users overlook
- Small code patterns that prevent a concrete everyday mistake
- Quick decision rules: “use X when this, Y when that”

## Reject these

- The article topic rewritten as a thread
- “Complete guide,” “deep dive,” architecture lesson, or multi-step course
- Generic productivity advice or motivation
- Tool listicles without one sharp payoff
- News, launches, drama, or facts that will expire quickly
- A claim that needs several paragraphs of context before it becomes useful
- Trivia with no practical or genuinely interesting consequence
- Advice so broad it could apply to any tool or job

## How to pick

Draft at least 5 candidates. For each, write:

- the exact one-line payoff
- what the viewer can do or understand in under a minute
- why they would save or share it

Pick the candidate with the clearest immediate payoff, then check `social_topic` history for duplicates and near-duplicates. The social topic should also be materially different from today's article topic.

## Research gate

Use 3 planned searches:

1. the official documentation, specification, or original source
2. a practical example or reliable demonstration
3. the most common confusion, mistake, or limitation

Keep 2–4 sources. Stop when you can state the claim accurately, show the useful action, and name the important limitation in plain language.
